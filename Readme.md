## 📌 Project Overview

Digital devices make information, entertainment, and communication accessible at any moment. However, the relationship between people and their devices can become difficult to manage.

This project explores the intersection of **digital well-being, attention, and mental wellness**, with an emphasis on understanding the experiences of people who struggle with excessive phone use, digital distractions, and feelings of mental overload.

Rather than treating the problem as a simple lack of discipline, the project investigates the behavioral patterns, emotional experiences, and environmental factors that may influence people's relationships with technology.

The goal is to explore whether a thoughtfully designed digital experience can help people develop greater awareness of their habits and make more intentional choices.

## 🎯 The Problem Space

Technology is designed to capture attention, simplify tasks, and keep people engaged. Yet, for some people, frequent digital engagement may conflict with their intentions to focus, rest, connect with others, or pursue meaningful activities.

This creates an important design question:

**How might we help people build a healthier relationship with their digital habits without making them feel guilty, overwhelmed, or controlled?**

This question forms the starting point of the project.

It is a research question, not a conclusion about every user's experience.

### Areas of Exploration

The project investigates several related areas:

* **Digital habits:** Understanding when and why people repeatedly reach for their phones.
* **Attention and focus:** Exploring the relationship between digital distractions and people's attempts to concentrate.
* **Emotional experiences:** Investigating whether boredom, loneliness, stress, or other emotional states influence digital behavior.
* **Self-awareness:** Exploring how people recognize patterns in their own behavior.
* **Behavior change:** Investigating what might help people make intentional changes without relying entirely on willpower.
* **Digital well-being:** Exploring how an app can support healthier habits without becoming another source of screen time.

These are research directions, not confirmed user insights.

## 👥 Who Is This For?

The initial audience under consideration is young adults who feel that their digital habits sometimes interfere with their attention, daily activities, or personal goals.

Potential participants may have different experiences with:

* Frequent scrolling and difficulty disengaging from digital content.
* Distractions while studying or working.
* Feeling mentally overloaded by continuous digital consumption.
* Difficulty maintaining healthier routines.
* Wanting to use technology more intentionally.

These groups are starting points for research, not finalized personas.

An important objective of the research is to determine whether these experiences share a meaningful underlying problem or represent different problems that require different solutions.

## 🔎 Research and Discovery

The project begins with understanding people before deciding what the product should do.

Instead of assuming that everyone who spends excessive time on their phone has the same motivation, the research aims to explore the context behind their behavior.

### Research Questions

The discovery phase focuses on questions such as:

1. In what situations do people find it difficult to disengage from their phones?
2. What are people trying to achieve when they turn to digital content?
3. How do people describe the effects of these habits on their everyday activities?
4. What strategies have they already tried, and what happened?
5. What would make a digital well-being tool useful rather than intrusive?
6. How do people define a healthier relationship with technology for themselves?

### Research Methods

The research approach may include:

* **User interviews:** Understanding people's experiences, motivations, and existing coping strategies.
* **Surveys:** Exploring patterns across a broader group of participants.
* **Competitive analysis:** Examining how existing products approach screen time, focus, habit formation, and digital well-being.
* **Research synthesis:** Organizing evidence into themes, behavioral patterns, and potential design opportunities.

Research methods and activities will be documented as they are conducted.

No research findings should be considered validated until they are supported by collected evidence.

## 🧠 Product Thinking

An important part of this project is exploring the difference between addressing a visible behavior and understanding the problem behind it.

For example, reducing screen time may be a useful outcome for some people. For others, the underlying challenge may involve distraction, boredom, stress, habit formation, or the way digital activities fit into their daily lives.

These possibilities should not be treated as interchangeable.

The product direction will be informed by research into the following questions:

* Is excessive phone use the primary problem, or is it a symptom of a different challenge?
* Which user group experiences the clearest and most significant problem?
* What kind of support would people actually find useful?
* How can the experience encourage intentional behavior without creating unnecessary pressure?
* How will the product know whether it is helping users?

The objective is to identify a focused problem before committing to a particular feature set.

## 💡 Design Principles Under Exploration

The following principles represent the intended direction of the design exploration. They will need to be evaluated against research findings and usability testing.

### 01. Empathy Over Judgment

Avoid treating people's digital habits as a moral failure. Explore ways to support awareness and change without unnecessary guilt or shame.

### 02. Awareness Before Intervention

Help users understand their own patterns before encouraging them to change those patterns.

### 03. Simplicity Over Overload

Keep interactions focused and understandable, especially when the product is intended for people who may already feel distracted or mentally overloaded.

### 04. User Agency Over Control

Explore ways to help users make their own decisions rather than relying exclusively on restrictive controls, pressure, or rigid routines.

### 05. Meaningful Engagement Over More Screen Time

Evaluate whether each interaction provides genuine value. A digital well-being product should not maximize engagement at the expense of the user's broader goals.

## 🛠️ Design Process

The project follows a human-centered design approach.

| Stage     | Focus                                                   | Intended Output                                         |
| :-------- | :------------------------------------------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------ |
| Empathize | Understand users, their contexts, and their experiences | Research evidence and behavioral observations           |
| Define    | Identify and frame the core problem                     | Evidence-based problem statement and research synthesis |
| Ideate    | Explore possible ways to address the problem            | Concepts, alternatives, and prioritized opportunities   |
| Prototype | Translate selected ideas into testable experiences      | Wireframes and interactive Figma prototypes             |
| Test      | Evaluate usability and learn from users                 | Usability findings and design iterations                |

The process is iterative. Findings from testing may require revisiting earlier assumptions or design decisions.

The emphasis is not on completing screens as quickly as possible, but on establishing a clear connection between user evidence, product decisions, and the resulting experience.

## 🛡️ Responsible Design Considerations

Designing for mental wellness requires particular attention to user trust, privacy, and the potential effects of product decisions.

This project will consider:

* **Privacy:** Collecting only the information necessary for the product's intended purpose.
* **Transparency:** Making the purpose of data collection and personalization understandable.
* **User control:** Giving users meaningful control over their information and interactions.
* **Non-judgmental feedback:** Avoiding unnecessary shame, guilt, or punitive feedback around personal habits.
* **Appropriate boundaries:** Distinguishing general digital well-being support from professional mental-health care.
* **Accessibility:** Considering different abilities, circumstances, and interaction needs.

These considerations will inform design decisions as the product direction becomes clearer.

The application is not intended to diagnose mental-health conditions or replace professional care.

Beyond producing a mobile interface, this project is an opportunity to develop stronger product design skills.

* Conducting research before committing to solutions.
